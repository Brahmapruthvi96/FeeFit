FeeFit

Personalised banking: finding which fees push customers away, and pricing each customer fairly.

01 · The problem

Same fee. Different customers.
One closed account.

COST

Maintenance charges, debit card fees and penalties are the same for everyone, though a ₹300 fee means very different things to different people.

IRRITATION

Small, repeated charges feel like being nickel-and-dimed, and they spoil an otherwise fine banking experience.

EXIT

Customers rarely close loudly. Balances drain and salary credits move first, so closures understate the damage.

Which fees push which customers away, and what is the best fee for each type?

02 · Real cases and issues

This is not hypothetical.

SBI’s minimum balance U-turn

SBI scrapped the requirement in 2012, brought it back in April 2017 and faced heavy criticism. It collected over ₹2,400 crore in penalties in 2017–18, then removed the penalty in March 2020 along with SMS charges.

The News Minute

Charges on zero-balance accounts

Moneylife reported SBI collected about ₹300 crore in service charges from zero-balance accounts during 2015–20, and later refunded charges on digital transactions.

Moneylife

Public banks drop the penalty

In 2025 more public sector banks, including Canara, PNB, Indian Bank and Bank of Baroda, removed the penalty after internal studies. Such penalties were about 0.3% of public banks’ revenue in FY24.

Free Press Journal

Still earning from penalties

Parliament was told in July 2026 that banks collected over ₹7,086 crore in FY26 from minimum balance penalties, with private banks about 70% of it.

Swarajya

Fees and switching, worldwide

A J.D. Power study found US customers were more than twice as likely to switch banks after being charged any fee in the previous three months. Capital One and Ally dropped overdraft fees in 2021.

Banking Dive

Lesson: banks keep reversing one-size-fits-all rules after the damage is done. FeeFit measures the tolerance first.

03 · How FeeFit works

Measure the fee each customer type can tolerate.

01IDENTIFY

Cluster customers by behaviour (balance, usage, tenure) with K-Means. Age, gender and religion are never used.

02MEASURE

OLS regression estimates each segment’s fee-tolerance point: the fee level where churn risk starts to jump.

03PRESCRIBE

Net-value rule: pick the fee that maximises (1 − churn risk) × (fee + retained customer margin).

04CHECK

Fairness audit: compare fees and waiver rates across gender and geography and flag large gaps.

05 · Research and proof

What the research says.

Direct proof

Price drives switching

Zhao, Noman & Asiaei (2022), Int. J. Bank Marketing 40(2). Price is one of the significant drivers of retail bank switching (Malaysia).

doi.org/10.1108/IJBM-01-2021-0042
Direct proof

Indian customers

Agarwal (2019), J. Marketing & Consumer Research 54. Survey of 450 customers of nine Indian banks finds price among eight switching drivers.

doi.org/10.7176/JMCR/54-01
Direct proof

Switching in China

Clemes, Gan & Zhang (2010), Int. J. Bank Marketing 28(7). Empirical framework for why retail bank customers switch.

doi.org/10.1108/02652321011085185
Near the concept

Personalised pricing can backfire

Lu, Xu, Dai & Zhou (2020), SSRN. In a grocery platform, personalised dynamic pricing raised churn as well as spending, so FeeFit tests rather than assumes.

doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3658362
Near the concept

Fees are a trigger, not the only cause

J.D. Power (2012), via The Financial Brand: fees are a contributing factor for customers already dissatisfied, which is why segmentation matters.

The Financial Brand
Near the concept

Blanket cuts don’t pay

Industry analysis (Financial Brand, Bain) finds that dropping fees for everyone costs income with little retention gain. Fee income can reach up to 40% of revenue at some banks (McKinsey, via Infosys).

The Financial Brand
06 · Developments

How we got here.

01PROBLEM

Started from small-business cash flow, judged it too vague, and searched for sharper, finance-focused questions.

02CONCEPT

Chose banks and customer churn, then framed the fee question as personalised banking. Approved by our professor.

03EVIDENCE

Collected papers, industry studies and real incidents (sections 02 and 05).

04METHOD

Designed the four stages: K-Means, OLS, net-value rule, fairness audit.

05DELIVERABLES

Website, presentation and research paper. Analysis code on the bank churn dataset comes next.
